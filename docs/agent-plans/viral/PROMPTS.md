# Research prompts

Paste each into a web-search assistant (the owner uses Gemini). Put the answers, with their sources, into RESEARCH.md beside this page.
Each prompt asks for sources and dates, because the field moves fast and marketing copy is common.

## 1. Open-source browser games we may build on

> List open-source 3D browser games built with three.js, Babylon.js or PlayCanvas that are playable today, with a shooter, racing,
> fighting or football/sports mechanic, and that are released under MIT, Apache 2.0 or BSD licences (not GPL or "source available").
> For each: the repository link, licence, last commit date, whether it has multiplayer, whether its assets are also openly licensed,
> approximate size of the built game, and whether it runs well on a mid-range Android phone. Separate code licence from asset licence.
> Give sources and dates.

## 2. Free 3D assets we can legally use

> List the best sources of 3D game assets (models, textures, animations, sounds) that are free for commercial use with no royalties:
> CC0 or CC BY. Include Kenney, Quaternius, Poly Haven, Poly Pizza, Sketchfab's CC0 filter, OpenGameArt, Mixamo (state Adobe's exact
> terms), and any others. For each: licence, whether attribution is required and how, typical polygon counts (we need low-poly for
> phones), and whether there are packs for cars, characters, weapons, stadiums and city props. Give sources.

## 3. Blender MCP, honestly

> What can the open-source Blender MCP add-on (the MIT one on GitHub) actually do for game asset production in 2026? Find real user
> reports, not marketing: quality of generated low-poly props and characters, how much cleanup (scale, orientation, materials, poly
> count) they need before use in a web game, whether it can export glTF directly, what it costs when it calls Hyper3D or Hunyuan3D, and
> the security warnings Blender itself gives about running model-generated code. Compare with Blender Open MCP (local models via
> Ollama). Give sources and dates.

## 4. How the big platforms let others build inside them

> Explain, with sources, how Roblox, Fortnite Creative (UEFN) and similar platforms let outside builders publish games inside the
> platform: the contract a game must meet, how identity, friends, currency and leaderboards are shared, how games are reviewed, how
> creators are paid, and what their revenue shares are. Then list open-source or small platforms that tried the same in the browser
> and what happened to them. We want to know what a "game cartridge" standard needs to cover.

## 5. Agent arenas and AI leagues

> Find existing projects where AI agents compete against each other for people to watch: agent arenas, AI leagues, agent tournaments
> (for games, trading, or simulated life), including any that let a user plug in their own assistant through the Model Context
> Protocol or an API key. For each: what the agents compete at, how fairness and cheating are handled, how spectators watch, how the
> results are shared, how big the audience got, and whether it still runs. Give sources and dates.

## 6. Connecting a user's own assistant

> As of now, exactly how does a user connect an external MCP server to ChatGPT (custom connectors or Developer Mode: which plans,
> which menu, what the form asks for, whether write actions need confirmation), to Claude (desktop and web), to Gemini, and to Cursor?
> Is there any form of one-click "add to assistant" link for any of them? Give the official documentation links and the date of each.

## 7. Short video ads that work, per platform and per country

> For TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, WhatsApp status and X: the best-performing formats for mobile browser games in 2025
> and 2026 (length, first-two-seconds hooks, captions, sound-on vs sound-off, calls to action), with any published numbers. Then the
> same for Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, the UK and the US specifically: which platform people under 30 use most for games, and
> any case studies of browser games that grew through WhatsApp status or TikTok in Africa. Give sources and dates.

## 8. What made social worlds spread

> Give five documented cases of social or life-simulation games that spread by word of mouth, and five that had every feature but did
> not. For each: what the sharable moment was, what the first-week retention looked like if published, what the "friend" mechanic was,
> and what killed the ones that failed. Prefer post-mortems written by the teams themselves. Include at least two African or South
> Asian cases. Give sources and dates.

## 9. Clans, seasons and ladders that keep people

> What is known, with data, about clans/guilds, seasons and ranked ladders and retention in mobile games? What sizes of clan work,
> what season length, what happens to players who lose rank, and what mistakes make ladders toxic. Include anything measured on
> low-end Android and on prepaid data. Give sources.

## 10. Programmatic video production

> What open-source tools let a developer generate many short vertical videos programmatically from recordings of a web game plus
> captions and motion graphics (for example Remotion, FFmpeg-based pipelines, Playwright video capture), what they cost to run, and
> what quality people get from them? Give repository links, licences and examples of ads made with them.
