**Allworld: reasons to return, achievements, and a world shared with AI**

Research date: 14 October 2026, Africa/Lagos. Product research and source audit; no gameplay implementation or production change. The comparison includes 134 core game-award winners, seven separately labelled prototype/showcase/category-honour cases, and 12 historical comparators from 1972–1997. Whiteout Survival and Candy Crush receive additional focused treatment. The companion catalogue contains the game-by-game evidence, award checks, return-loop analysis, human-desire mapping, and transfer ideas. Numerical proposals below are starting designs to validate, not measured optimal values.

**The recommendation**

Make Allworld a place where a human can build a life worth showing: become known for something, have relationships that remember their actions, finish stories, improve a home, and leave a visible mark on a neighbourhood. Every short visit should offer one enjoyable action and a comprehensible next step. Over time, those actions should produce a history the player owns.

AI agents should help the world respond: a familiar resident remembers a promise, a rival presents a challenge, a missing teammate can be replaced, and an event can function when few humans are online. Human choices must still determine the meaningful outcomes. The marketing promise is the life and the experiences people can have. The agent technology supports that promise.

Allworld already has a substantial progression foundation. Its source includes daily and weekly missions, a weekly activity card, titles, starter goals, wishes, dreams, perks, careers, NPC memory, home scenes, campus progression, games, and separate agent rankings. The highest-value work is to make these systems coherent, improve the actions beneath them, and add durable achievements and consequential episodes. Building another disconnected checklist would increase complexity without establishing a stronger reason to return.

**How to use this research**

Read this document for the decision and proposed system. Use [the game catalogue](retention-catalogue-2026-10-14/CATALOGUE.md) for the extensive comparative list and [the source inventory](retention-source-inventory-2026-10-14.md) for the implementation evidence. [The focused Whiteout/Candy Crush/Clash/Fortnite note](retention-game-evidence-2026-10-14.md) provides additional mechanic details and version caveats.

The comparison deliberately includes ongoing multiplayer games, games with strong replay loops, and finite games with compelling continuation. These solve different problems. A wonderful finite narrative is evidence about attachment and anticipation, not proof of months of daily retention. An award proves a named form of recognition, not that a game is addictive, commercially successful, or retained a particular percentage of players. No comparable private retention datasets or causal experiments for these games were available.

Mechanic descriptions are sourced facts where the linked material supports them. Explanations of why they may motivate a return and recommendations for Allworld are analysis. This is a broad comparative design review, not a systematic causal study or a ranking of games by addiction. Platform, specialist, audience, editorial, and major industry awards are identified by their actual names rather than treated as equivalent.

**What the Whiteout screenshots show**

The supplied Growth Missions image separates a main upgrade objective from side objectives. It shows a specific next requirement, current progress, visible rewards, and either Go or Claim. Completing an objective changes what the player can do on the same row. This reduces the effort required to decide what comes next.

The Daily Missions image layers individual task rewards underneath an overall activity meter. The displayed thresholds are 40, 80, 120, 160, 215, 270, and 325; displayed progress is 230. The next visible threshold is therefore 40 points away. Several different activities advance the same larger target. The countdown establishes a daily cadence. These exact numbers are evidence from the user's screenshots, not a claim about every Whiteout account or version.

The useful pattern is that each action has several meanings: complete something now, move a larger bar, and prepare for the next milestone. Allworld can borrow that structure without copying the thresholds, visual design, resource inflation, or time pressure. Century Games also documents seven-stage anniversary missions and collective alliance contributions, showing longer and social horizons alongside the daily layer. **What the strongest patterns have in common**

Players need both an enjoyable action and a reason that action matters. A clear task helps someone start; responsive feedback makes the action satisfying; visible improvement creates competence; choice makes the progress personal; recognition makes it shareable; relationships make returning matter to someone else. A reward schedule can reinforce this loop, but cannot supply all of it.

Research by Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski found associations between perceived autonomy, competence, relatedness, enjoyment, and future play across four studies. This supports looking beyond rewards to choice, mastery, and social connection. It does not establish the effect of a particular daily quest design in Allworld. A later review also cautions against treating this theoretical framework as an unquestioned explanation for every game. [Original research](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf), [HCI research review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12639).

There are five distinct reasons to return that Allworld should support:

| Return motive | The player's thought | Suitable Allworld expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mastery | I can do that better next time. | A table challenge, a route, a job dilemma, a skill-specific task. |
| Completion | I am close to finishing something I care about. | An aspiration chapter, collection, home project, or achievement tier. |
| Anticipation | I want to see what happens next. | A resident follow-up, a new episode, or the result of a decision. |
| Belonging | These are my people and my place. | Recurring friends, a neighbourhood project, a group tradition. |
| Expression | This is becoming mine. | A recognisable character, decorated home, chosen title, and history. |

Daily missions are one delivery mechanism for these motives. They are not a sixth substitute for them.

**The fundamental human desires underneath the mechanics**

Start with the experience a person wants: “I can improve,” “I can choose,” “I belong,” “I matter,” or “this is mine.” Then choose a game action that can deliver that experience. A streak, badge, or progress bar is a way to communicate or reinforce it. A person can collect a badge without feeling competent, or complete a social mission without feeling connected.

Autonomy, competence, and relatedness are the three basic psychological needs in Self-Determination Theory. The additional motives below are useful design lenses, not a scientifically established nine-level hierarchy. Players can value several at once, and priorities can change between sessions. Yee's online-game research likewise distinguishes achievement, social, and immersion motivations rather than reducing all play to rewards. [Game motivation study](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf), [Yee's original paper](https://www.nickyee.com/pubs/Yee%20-%20Motivations%20%282006%29.pdf). The [research evidence memo](human-desires-evidence-2026-10-14.md) records ten sources, access limits, and what each does and does not establish.

| Human desire | What the player wants to feel | Game patterns that can serve it | Concrete Allworld expression | How the same feature can fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mastery / competence | I am getting better at something. | Learnable challenges, feedback, personal bests, mastery badges; Tetris, Portal, Hades, Rocket League. | A skill-specific venue challenge with practice, a visible improvement record, and an earned demonstration badge. | Repetition is called skill; an agent or purchased boost earns the same mastery mark. |
| Autonomy / agency | My choices shape this experience. | Multiple routes, strategies, builds, self-set projects; Zelda, Civilization, Baldur's Gate 3. | Choose an ambition and solve a resident problem through two genuinely different approaches. | Every option resolves identically, or daily tasks dictate the entire session. |
| Belonging / care | I have people and a place here. | Stable groups, complementary cooperation, recurring characters; World of Warcraft, It Takes Two, Sky. | A small crew, a familiar resident, a shared weekly tradition, and remembered promises. | Fake human presence, compulsory invites, or guilt for missing a group task. |
| Esteem / recognition | Something I did deserves to be noticed. | Skill ranks, distinctive titles, public accomplishments; competitive games and cooperative team feats. | Several routes to recognition: a helpful neighbour, excellent host, skilled player, or accomplished creator. | A single wealth leaderboard makes everyone else invisible; bots dominate comparisons. |
| Curiosity / discovery | I want to know what is there or what happens next. | Clues, new possibilities, mysteries, exploration; Outer Wilds, Obra Dinn, early Zelda. | A clue in a district, a resident follow-up, a choice with a visible consequence on the next visit. | Endless teasing, arbitrary locks, or mysteries with no satisfying answer. |
| Identity / expression | This reflects who I am or who I want to try being. | Role-play, avatar design, home building, chosen affiliations; The Sims, Animal Crossing, RPGs. | Character style, a chosen aspiration, three displayed badges, home objects with personal stories. | Everyone is funnelled toward the same optimal identity or generic cosmetic ladder. |
| Ownership / stewardship | My effort leaves something lasting that I can care for. | Persistent creations, collections, ongoing worlds; SimCity, Minecraft, Animal Crossing. | A home project, passport collection, saved scene, or neighbourhood improvement that remains visible. | A reset erases earned work, an agent finishes it without the human, or upkeep becomes punishment. |
| Purpose / contribution | My actions help or change something that matters here. | Consequential choices, interdependent roles, repair/care narratives; Spiritfarer, Death Stranding, cooperative games. | A completed project changes a shared venue; the contribution ledger shows the player's actual role. | A global bar rises but nothing in the world changes, or contribution is merely donated currency. |
| Comfort / continuity | I can return, understand the situation, and enjoy my place. | Familiar rituals, readable rules, gentle re-entry, reliable saves; cosy simulation and persistent-world patterns. | “Here is where you left off,” a forgiving weekly card, saved rewards, an inviting home, optional reminders. | Broken streaks, lost claims, a wall of expired chores, or aggressive notifications make return unpleasant. |

The game-to-desire labels in the catalogue are analytical interpretations of documented mechanics. They are not measured psychological profiles of each game's audience. Completion, scarcity, anticipation, random rewards, and countdowns are mechanisms; status, mastery, connection, and agency are possible motives. Keep these levels separate when deciding what to build.

For example, a neighbourhood festival can serve several desires without requiring several disconnected reward systems. A human chooses a role (autonomy), performs a learnable task (competence), coordinates with another player (belonging), helps open the venue (contribution), receives a commemorative object (ownership), and displays it at home (identity and recognition). A returning resident remembers what happened (continuity). An AI can provide an absent role or react to the result, while the human's contribution remains attributable.

Streaks principally make continuity and commitment visible. A daily attendance count does not establish mastery. Achievements should record an accomplishment; badges should communicate something worth knowing about it; missions should offer an appealing next action. These distinctions prevent a single activity counter from pretending to satisfy every desire.

Two additional research findings are useful with limits. Curiosity research describes an information gap between what a person knows and wants to know; this suggests specific questions with eventual answers. Goal-gradient studies found increased effort near rewards in consumer programmes; this suggests testing legible, nearby milestones. Neither proves that a game needs more locked content or more countdowns. [Loewenstein's curiosity research](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf), [Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng](https://drupalgsb-dev.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf).

Reward design also needs context. A meta-analysis found that some expected contingent rewards reduced later free-choice engagement, while positive feedback could support interest. This is a reason to make badges informative and personally meaningful; it is not a claim that every game reward is harmful. [Deci, Koestner and Ryan](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan_Meta.pdf).

**What the comparison across time reveals**

The periods below are sampling lenses, not a claim that one form of game design replaced another. Arcade mastery, stories, social worlds, and scheduled events coexist today. The 12 older cards have verified original release dates; contemporary catalogue dates are the cited award years, not release years. Retrospective museum inductions are kept separate from contemporary awards. [Historical evidence and cards](retention-catalogue-2026-10-14/historical-context.md).

| Historical lens | Representative examples | Recurring motivation | What Allworld can take from it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1970s–early 1980s arcade play | Pong, Space Invaders, Pac-Man | Immediate control, clear feedback, improvement, rematches, social comparison. | A small action should be enjoyable enough to repeat before it has a daily reward attached. |
| Mid-1980s–1990s exploration and creation | Tetris, Super Mario Bros., Zelda, SimCity, Civilization, Super Mario 64 | Learning, discovery, personal plans, persistent progress. | Build a comprehensible world with interesting decisions and saved projects. Give players room to choose. |
| Late-1990s–2000s persistent and social worlds | Ultima Online, The Sims, World of Warcraft, competitive multiplayer | An enduring persona, relationships, community roles, long projects. | A return should reconnect the person with their history and people. Persistence itself is valuable. |
| 2010s mobile and live operations | Candy Crush, Clash of Clans, Pokémon GO, Fortnite | Convenient sessions, visible next targets, collections, recurring events. | Organise the return cadence and reduce decision friction around a worthwhile underlying activity. |
| 2010s–2020s narrative, cooperative and systemic design | Hades, Outer Wilds, It Takes Two, Baldur's Gate 3, Balatro | New combinations, consequential choices, shared problem-solving, mysteries and replay. | Use variation, relationships, and consequences to make the next visit meaningfully different. |

The synthesis is that Allworld needs three connected layers: an enjoyable action, a life that accumulates meaning, and a convenient invitation to return. Daily missions and streaks chiefly organise the third layer. Achievements, relationships, homes, stories, and community consequences help connect all three. AI should expand the situations and responses available within those layers.

**Comparative lessons most relevant to Allworld**

| Reference | Documented pattern | Design inference for Allworld |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whiteout Survival | Main/side objectives, daily activity thresholds in the supplied screenshots; staged alliance events in publisher material. | Keep the next action legible and let different actions contribute to a common weekly result. |
| Candy Crush Saga | The documented Daily Treat Machine has six five-day tiers; rewards improve across tiers, with a possible setback after a missed day. Episode Race overlays competition on normal level completion. | Show the next benefit clearly. Separate a skill win streak from a calendar streak and avoid making reconnect failures destroy earned progress. | Clash of Clans | Clan Games turn individual challenges into shared reward tiers, with individual participation requirements. | Let different human roles contribute to a visible neighbourhood outcome; cap each person's contribution so the event is not decided by one grinder. [Clan Games](https://support.supercell.com/clash-of-clans/en/articles/clan-games-rewards.html). |
| Fortnite: Save the World | Epic's 2023 redesign moved value from login rewards into quests and allowed up to three daily quests to be banked. | Reward meaningful play and provide a catch-up path. This is a historical Save the World example, not a claim that every Fortnite mode has identical rules. [Epic's redesign](https://www.fortnite.com/news/changes-coming-to-fortnite-save-the-worlds-daily-reward-system-in-v25-10). |

[Nintendo's island guide](https://animalcrossing.nintendo.com/new-horizons/explore/). |
| Sky: Children of the Light | Official documentation describes varied daily quest categories and seasonal rewards. | A social objective, an exploration objective, and an individual objective can offer variety, provided solo alternatives exist. | Hades, Returnal, FTL, Balatro | Run-based variation, learning, and persistent or discoverable possibilities; individual evidence is in the catalogue. | Repeated play should create a different decision or teach something. Repeating the same timer with a larger target is a weak substitute. |
| Baldur's Gate 3, Mass Effect 2, Disco Elysium | Choices, character builds, relationships, and narrative consequences. | Let a player recognise a later consequence of an earlier decision. A remembered deed can be more valuable than another cash grant. |
| It Takes Two, Overcooked, Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes | Complementary roles and interdependent cooperation. | Make humans useful to one another through different contributions, with a way to play when friends are absent. |
| Rocket League, competitive shooters, fighting games | Repeated skill contests and legible improvement. | Keep personal bests and fair brackets separate from wealth, account age, and agent automation. |

**A comprehensive mechanic catalogue**

This is a menu of design tools, not a proposal to ship every row. The examples refer to the companion game catalogue. Priorities mean: Start = first coherent release; Next = after that loop works; Later = requires evidence or substantial content; Avoid = poor fit for the human-first direction.

| # | Mechanic | Why it can motivate another visit | Allworld application | Priority / failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One clear next action | Removes uncertainty about how to start. | Resume the selected ambition or nearest valid mission. | Start; do not show six competing tutorials. |
| 2 | Progress counters | Makes distance to completion concrete. | 2 of 3 distinct neighbourhood stops completed. | Start; match the counter to what the text promises. |
| 3 | Progress thresholds | Supplies several achievable finish points. | First, second, and third daily activity milestones. | Next; avoid a mandatory full bar. |
| 4 | Reward previews | Lets the player choose a worthwhile goal. | Show the actual souvenir, title, or unlock before starting. | Start; mystery rewards are not a replacement for value. |
| 5 | Immediate action feedback | Makes input feel consequential. | A resident reacts, a repair appears, a skill result is shown. | Start; a toast alone can be too weak. |
| 6 | Short complete activities | Lowers the effort needed to return. | A two-to-five-minute challenge with an ending. | Start; shorter waiting is not necessarily better play. |
| 7 | Player-selected objectives | Gives ownership over progress. | Pick an ambition and swap a daily prompt. | Start; prevent an overwhelmingly large choice screen. |
| 8 | Eligibility-aware missions | Keeps the next goal achievable. | Only offer supported locations, affordable routes, and available activities. | Start; an impossible task breaks trust. |
| 9 | Daily variety | Changes the invitation while retaining familiar rules. | Rotate activity families and resident contexts. | Next; changing the name alone is not variety. |
| 10 | Weekly goals | Supports people who cannot play daily. | Four active days and a multi-session project. | Start; no seven-day obligation. |

| 14 | Delayed consequences | Creates a specific reason to revisit. | A resident follows up after a promise was kept. | Next; show timing and do not require a narrow appointment. |
| 15 | Permanent achievements | Makes effort survive changing events. | Named milestones with evidence and completion dates. | Start; avoid hundreds of identical counters. |

| 20 | Home trophies | Places accomplishment inside the world. | A framed chapter result or a project plaque. | Start; keep rendering cost controlled. |
| 21 | Outfits and expressive cosmetics | Makes progress recognisable to others. | Earned garment, emote, or accessory related to an activity. | Next; assets must work on the canonical character. |

| 24 | Build specialisation | Encourages experimentation and identity. | Choose role emphasis using existing traits/perks. | Later; preserve an affordable change of direction. |
| 25 | Challenge modifiers | Reuses content with meaningful differences. | A delivery with a budget constraint, or a harder table rule. | Next; do not simply increase repetitions. |
| 26 | Replay with variation | Makes another attempt informative. | Different request combinations and authored choices. | Next; prevent infinite unbalanced reward farming. |

| 32 | Return recap | Restores context after a break. | What changed, what is preserved, and one useful action. | Start; do not greet someone with a bill wall. |

| 46 | Relevant opt-in reminders | Helps someone remember a chosen activity. | One reminder for an event or a story they followed. | Later; respect quiet hours and easy opt-out. |
| 47 | Low-friction re-entry | Removes the cost of resuming. | Restore place, state, selected goal, and interrupted context. | Start; slow loading can negate a good progression design. |
| 48 | Transparent rewards and accounting | Makes achievement trustworthy. | Completion receipt, saved unlock, clear claim status. | Start; never ask the client or an LLM to decide a payout. |
| 49 | Bounded convenience rewards | Makes accomplishment useful without unlimited power. | A limited route voucher or an extra cosmetic choice. | Later; model effects on economy and other players. |
| 50 | Optional aspirational prestige | Gives experts a long horizon. | A difficult mastery title with no economic multiplier. | Later; keep beginners relevant. |
| 51 | Human/AI contribution provenance | Preserves meaning in a mixed world. | Record who performed each competitive or social contribution. | Start before new competitive rewards. |
| 52 | AI as an available collaborator | Helps a session happen in a quiet city. | An identified substitute or resident request giver. | Next; never claim it is a human friend. |
| 53 | AI-driven flavour within fixed rules | Varies context without changing the contract. | Different resident dialogue around a validated mission template. | Later; keep a deterministic playable fallback. |
| 54 | World consequences that persist | Makes a place feel changed by its inhabitants. | A mural, noticeboard, event display, or resident opportunity. | Next; bound the data and asset cost. |
| 55 | Meaningful economy sinks | Makes earnings support aspirations. | Home, transport, style, and optional projects. | Audit first; do not balance through punitive poverty. |
| 56 | Multiple satisfying stopping points | Lets a good session end voluntarily. | One task, a small daily milestone, or a complete episode. | Start; an unfinished full bar must not imply failure. |
| 57 | Random paid power rewards | Can encourage repeated acquisition attempts. | Poor fit for the first progression release. | Avoid; undermines fair human/agent competition. |
| 58 | Paid streak repair | Converts fear of losing progress into spending. | Do not use as the return loop. | Avoid; free recovery better supports trust. |
| 59 | Endless escalation of daily chores | Keeps targets out of reach. | Do not increase required play with tenure. | Avoid; established players should gain depth, not obligation. |
| 60 | Fabricated social urgency | Uses simulated people to pressure a return. | Never invent human demand or guilt-laden resident distress. | Avoid; identify agents and offer real choices. |

| Telemetry | Session, active-day, mission, table, social, invitation, and performance events exist. | Extend the existing catalogue with missing completion/outcome/provenance events. No current retention dashboard was audited. [Events](../../src/telemetry/events.ts:35). |

**The most consequential gaps**

1. **Earned mission rewards can disappear at rollover.** `roll()` replaces the previous daily/weekly set before a later claim. A completed-but-unclaimed mission therefore lapses too. Preserve an earned entitlement at completion, then let celebration/collection be a presentation choice. Credit or queue it durably and idempotently; do not grant the same reward twice after reconnect. [Rollover and claim](../../src/game/systems/missions.ts:100).
2. **The observed analytics name conflates completion and collection.** `mission_completed` is tracked after a successful claim in the UI. Separate mission offered, started, completed, reward granted, and reward viewed. Otherwise someone who finishes but never collects looks like a non-finisher. [Claim handler](../../src/app/features/growth/MissionsApp.vue:48).
3. **Activity is recognised in several separate systems.** Titles, wishes, skills, family streaks, Oro, campus records, and home scenes need a readable player history. A unified passport is a view over those systems, not permission to replace them all.
4. **Some task labels promise more than their matching rule establishes.** “Say hello to two people” listens to `npc.greeted` counts. The mission listener does not deduplicate the NPC id, while the social system emits that event for hello actions. Verify repeated greetings in the real flow, then enforce distinct recipients or change the wording. [Definition](../../src/game/content/missions.ts:59), [Listener](../../src/game/systems/missions.ts:219), [Emitter](../../src/game/systems/social.ts:554).
5. **A counter is not always a satisfying activity.** More meals, greetings, trips, and timed actions can create chores. Each new mission family should include a decision, discovery, performance challenge, or shared result.
6. **A one-time eligibility check is insufficient for a changing world.** Mission dealing checks selected prerequisites and venues. Affordability, hours, content readiness, changed circumstances, and reachable alternatives deserve explicit acceptance checks; this audit does not establish a specific runtime failure for every case. [Eligibility](../../src/game/systems/missions.ts:70).
7. **Long ambitions need a stronger narrative connection.** “Hostel Hustle” is a prior brief, not a confirmed complete branching storyline. Build one arc whose choices produce later observable differences. [Existing brief](../PROGRESSION-BRIEF.md).
8. **Shared contribution needs a visible outcome.** A progress meter should culminate in a changed venue, event, relationship opportunity, or commemorative item. A larger number alone is a weaker social reward.
9. **Agent exclusion is not a complete human-retention measurement policy.** The inspected growth route excludes agent-owned characters using `session.agent?.char`. That is narrower than proving every automated action on a human-owned character is excluded. A five-minute public label is also different from the durable ranking-week marker. [Growth filter](../../server/routes/growth.ts:89), [Agent action stamp](../../server/agents/service.ts:480).
10. **Global progress requires explicit ownership.** The server settles city records and normalises character state. Define which achievements belong to a character across cities, a specific city, or a single event. Review the existing character-transfer path before adding global totals; do not infer that separate city records imply separate player identities. [Settlement](../../server/life-service.ts:116).
11. **A daily win should survive poor connectivity.** Completed rewards should be recoverable. Oro explicitly keeps unfinished guesses in memory while finished results are saved; this is a distinct recovery limitation to inspect before promoting a perfect streak. [Oro persistence](../../server/growth/oro.ts:9).
12. **The foundation must be reachable and responsive.** Character loading, travel, action feedback, mobile readability, and save recovery determine whether progression can be enjoyed. Existing rendering work should be accepted on the actual playable path before it becomes a required route for a mission. This source audit did not verify current browser or physical-device performance.

**Proposed player-facing structure**

Use one entry called **My Life** or **Passport**, with four clear views: Today, My Story, Achievements, and My People. The world itself remains the main place to play. The journal explains progress and routes people back to the relevant activity.

Today shows one selected next action, three manageable mission choices, the next activity milestone, and the weekly card. My Story shows the selected ambition, the current chapter, the last consequential choice, and the next opportunity. Achievements shows earned marks, three pinned favourites, collections, and a home display preview. My People shows actual human friends separately from identified residents, relevant promises, and group projects. Show only the next few rewards by default; the full catalogue is optional.

An achievement should answer five questions: What did I do? Why does it matter? What did I earn? Where can I show it? What is the next interesting step? For example: “Neighbourhood Host — you completed a gathering with two other people; earned a wall plaque; next: host a different activity.” A raw “+10 points” answers only one of these.

**Missions and daily activity**

Keep the current three daily categories, but give the player a useful choice and one free swap. Start with three tasks, not a 20-row chore list. A social task must have a valid solo resident alternative; a human-specific achievement can remain separate and clearly labelled. Do not require spending, an invitation, a win against another human, or presence at a narrow time to finish the base daily track.

For a first experiment, each completed daily mission could contribute 20 activity points, producing milestones at 20, 40, and 60. These points are a temporary progress counter, not money or another spendable currency. The first milestone should be worthwhile, the second should feel like a satisfying session, and the third should be optional completion. Keep existing cash ceilings while testing reward presentation. Use cosmetic progress or a choice of decorative rewards for added milestones; price any extra mechanical benefit in the existing economy model.

The collection button can still provide anticipation and celebration, but completed entitlements must be preserved independently of that button. If automatic grant is used, the UI should say what arrived and offer “View rewards.” If a choice is required, keep the choice pending instead of deleting it at midnight. Period-bound cash must remain attributed to its earning period, not be counted again when viewed later.

Keep the four-of-seven weekly card. A later banking experiment can preserve up to two missed daily sets, capped and visible; do not introduce banking, streak bonuses, and bigger cash rewards in the same experiment. Preserve the current server reset initially and display the player's local reset time. A later global-clock change needs anti-double-claim rules and migration, rather than silently switching reward periods when someone travels.

**Achievements, badges, and long-term goals**

Launch with roughly 24 well-authored achievements across six paths, then expand based on what people actually pursue. That is a proposed content scope, not a target derived from the comparison. Avoid a hundred indistinguishable counters at launch.

| Path | Entry achievement | Intermediate achievement | Aspirational achievement | Reward language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explorer | Finish a local trail. | Complete three distinct district stories. | Finish a curated cross-city collection. | Passport stamp, route souvenir, explorer title. |
| Neighbour | Resolve a resident request. | Complete a relationship arc with a remembered consequence. | Help complete several different neighbourhood projects. | Resident memento, plaque, neighbour title. |
| Professional | Complete a first meaningful work milestone. | Earn a promotion and resolve a work dilemma. | Finish the chosen career arc. | Certificate, work accessory, professional title. |
| Homemaker | Complete a room project. | Publish and play a coherent home scene. | Create a collection of distinct personal spaces/scenes. | Display item, décor variant, curator title. |
| Player/host | Finish a table tutorial and match. | Complete a cooperative event in a chosen role. | Master a challenge or establish a recurring group activity. | Emote, game set, host title. |
| Student/creator | Finish an introductory campus or creative chapter. | Complete a semester/project with a meaningful choice. | Finish a multi-chapter ambition and show its outcome. | Certificate, commemorative item, graduate/creator title. |

Each achievement definition needs stable criteria, progress, eligibility, reward, scope, and a completion record. Tier upgrades should require different evidence: breadth, skill, contribution, or a meaningful outcome. “Greet 10, then 100, then 1,000 people” mostly rewards repetition. Reserve permanent prestige for things a player can explain in a sentence.

Badges should remain earned after a season ends. Let a player equip a small number and display selected trophies at home. Keep human mastery badges separate from assisted completion where the distinction matters. Never use a residence-confirmation or trust badge as a game achievement: those communicate different facts.

**Streaks: what should increase, exactly?**

Allworld already distinguishes cumulative active days from some activity-specific consecutive streaks. Preserve lifetime progress as the reliable foundation. If a cross-game daily streak is added, make it optional, count meaningful player activity, and use it to improve cosmetic progression rather than income, voting power, work pay, or competitive strength.

The following is a concrete experiment design, not a recommendation to ship before the basic loop works. “Collection XP” below means non-spendable progress toward an earned cosmetic collection. It is not existing stars, cash, skill XP, or another purchased currency. If that collection track is not built, launch recognition-only streak milestones first.

| Consecutive qualifying days | Proposed benefit | Permanent record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Normal collection progress, 1.00×. | First active day recorded. |
| 3–6 | 1.05× collection progress. | Three-day ribbon. |
| 7–13 | 1.10× collection progress. | Seven-day badge and a cosmetic choice. |
| 14–29 | 1.15× collection progress. | Two-week display variant. |
| 30+ | 1.20× maximum; no further rate increase. | Thirty-day title/frame; later milestones are commemorative. |

Apply the bonus to a fixed daily base allowance so playing longer cannot multiply it indefinitely. For an illustrative 300-point daily allowance, the maximum extra progress would be 60 points. Base seasonal completion must remain achievable through the four-day weekly rhythm without a perfect streak. Recheck the cosmetic schedule before adopting these exact numbers.

One completed qualifying activity can mark a day; merely logging in, opening a chest, or an agent working while the person is away cannot. One automatic free rest day can be earned after four active days, held to a maximum of one. A rest day preserves the chain without adding an active day. A service outage can freeze eligibility under a documented server policy. Missing more time resets only the optional current streak and its capped bonus; it never removes achievements, best streak, home items, completed chapters, or lifetime active days. No paid repair and no “your friends will suffer” message.

Keep a win streak local to its activity, with rules that name the opponent/bracket and disconnect treatment. Do not merge Oro, family calls, table wins, and general attendance into one ambiguous number. Offer a clear weekly card for people who prefer a less frequent rhythm. Compare both systems before deciding whether a daily streak is actually useful.

**Human and AI participation contract**

The other computer's agent-play work should remain the owner of agent connectivity and execution. The progression layer needs a stable contract with it, rather than a second agent runtime. The contract below is a proposed integration brief; it was not sent to another chat or external party.

| Actor | Useful contribution | Reward/recognition rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human playing directly | Chooses ambitions, performs challenges, makes social and story decisions. | Eligible for human activity records and appropriate human competitions. |
| Human playing alongside an identified AI | Participates in a shared scene or gets help learning. | Can earn participation/story rewards; mastery criteria state whether assistance is allowed. |
| Agent delegated to a human-owned character | Performs permitted tasks through existing controls. | Ordinary permitted character progression remains valid; automated time does not count as the human returning. Preserve existing ranking-family rules. |
| Independent agent character | Acts as a resident, rival, merchant, or competitor within the same game rules. | Agent achievements/boards; no fabricated human participation. |
| Authored NPC | Provides a dependable baseline story or solo partner. | Clearly presented as a game character; does not need an LLM for every interaction. |

Preserve both actor identity and character ownership. Record whether an action came through human controls or a known agent credential at the server boundary; a client-supplied “human” flag is insufficient. This is provenance of known execution paths, not perfect detection of all browser automation.

For mixed projects, distinguish human contribution, agent contribution, and authored NPC support. Use separate contribution caps and a published success rule. Agents may keep an event playable, but cannot silently complete the entire human challenge or take every scarce reward before people arrive. Do not reward transfers between one's own agents, repeated greetings, self-invites, or recursive agent-generated missions as unique social contribution.

An AI resident should create an interesting situation: a request with two valid approaches, a remembered favour, a rival strategy, or a changed reaction. It should not generate arbitrary currencies, eligibility, or rewards in prose. The engine validates a template, available location, prerequisites, costs, completion events, reward ceiling, and expiry. The model can supply bounded dialogue and select among approved contexts. If it is unavailable, an authored interaction must remain playable; do not let model latency block reward settlement.

Use consented in-game memory and structured outcome tags. Personal messages, private conversations, or sensitive information should not become quest material simply because the model can access them. Measure model cost and failure rate per meaningful human session; unlimited autonomous activity is not evidence of product success.

**One coherent first experience**

Use the existing “Hostel Hustle” idea as a candidate first story, with a non-student neighbourhood equivalent if the selected launch audience is broader. The previous cooperative-adventure research and this progression proposal fit together: an episode supplies the enjoyable session; the passport, relationships, home, and weekly project make that episode matter afterward. The prior marketing hypothesis remains a hypothesis, not an audience decision established by this audit. [Earlier product research](allworld-differentiation-and-marketing-2026-10-14.md).

Proposed first week:

| Visit | Experience | Lasting outcome | Reason to return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First visit | Meet a recurring resident; complete one small playable request; choose a direction. | First passport entry and a modest memento. | A recognisable next chapter, not just another login gift. |
| Second visit | Take a work/study/help decision. | A stored choice and a changed relationship response. | See the consequence and prepare for a shared event. |
| Third visit | Try a short mastery activity or prepare an event role. | Skill/role progress and a visible project contribution. | The event is approaching and the player has a part in it. |

A guest can encounter the introduction. A current student enters at the appropriate point instead of repeating admission. A solo player can finish. A late arrival has a catch-up route. A player who declines university can select another ambition. These are acceptance conditions for the proposed experience, not current runtime claims.

**Economy and progression rules**

Keep four different rewards distinct: money buys ordinary goods; stars buy the existing perks; achievements document what happened; cosmetics and relationship outcomes express who the character is becoming. A daily activity meter is a progress display. Do not introduce a spendable currency for every layer.

Before changing payouts, run the existing economy model against realistic 1-, 7-, and 30-day play patterns, including a missed week. Include starter goals, daily/weekly missions, wishes/perks, jobs, gigs, table rewards, referrals, business income, transfers, travel, rent, groceries, housing, and cars. Inspect both ordinary human time budgets and agent-assisted earning. The current configured mission maximum of ₦8,250/week must be considered with all other sources; it is not independently evidence of an inflation problem.

Reward choice can increase relevance without increasing total economic value. Let a completed chapter offer a choice among equally scoped cosmetics or a commemorative furnishing. State the reward before the final commitment. Avoid selling the repair of a deliberately broken streak. Keep basic recovery and social play accessible when a player is poor. Returning should resume an aspiration, not require clearing a large stack of punishment first.

**Implementation boundaries and data ownership**

Reuse the registry and existing wallet/claim patterns. Define a progression event with a stable event id, occurrence time, character id, city-qualified target, action type, actor provenance, and validated result. Completion, entitlement, granting, and viewing are different states. The durable transaction must couple the completion record and entitlement; retries, duplicate events, two devices, and a midnight transition must converge to one result.

Content definitions should identify id/version, scope, prerequisites, allowed events, required distinct targets, progress cap, reward definition, period policy, agent eligibility, and routing. Version active missions explicitly so changing a definition does not reinterpret a saved count halfway through the period. A retired task needs a replacement or preserved earned reward, not an unknown-id failure.

Keep enduring achievements and story choices at their intended character scope. Use city-qualified ids for local discoveries and a single reward owner for cross-city achievements. Review the current character/session transfer code before selecting storage; do not duplicate an account-wide passport into every city record. Existing players should receive achievements only where historical state proves the criterion. Label unreconstructable counters as tracking from launch rather than inventing history.

Keep the passport a projection over existing domains. Careers still own promotions, social owns relationships, tables own match results, and the wallet owns cash. The achievement system listens to validated outcomes and grants durable recognition. It should not recalculate career or table rules from UI text. A community project adds its own contribution ledger and bounded outcome state; start with one authored definition.

**Measurement: prove that humans want to come back**

The primary product outcome should be the number and proportion of human players who return on another day and complete a meaningful action. Report acquisition cohorts separately from already-active players. Agent sessions, automated background progress, reward-screen opens, and total simulation actions must not stand in for human retention.

| Measure | Definition | Decision it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | Landing humans who complete the first meaningful playable outcome; also report technical drop-off before it. | Whether the opening experience works. |
| D1 / D7 / D30 return | Fully observed cohorts returning on the specified day, with a consistent documented time basis. | Whether early interest persists. |
| Meaningful weekly return | Unique humans active on at least two separate days with validated play, not only login. | Whether the world earns repeat use. |
| Episode progression | Intro → first decision → chapter finish → next-session continuation. | Where the story loses people. |
| Achievement value | Earned → viewed → equipped/displayed → revisited. | Whether badges mean anything to players. |
| Daily mission quality | Offer → start → complete → reward grant, plus swap and impossible-task rates. | Whether tasks guide or obstruct play. |
| Streak recovery | Return after a missed day or broken streak, compared with a weekly-card cohort. | Whether the cadence helps or discourages return. |
| Social return | The same humans participate together on multiple days; distinguish AI-assisted groups. | Whether belonging is developing. |
| World consequence | Contributors revisit or use the changed venue/result. | Whether projects matter beyond the reward. |
| Economy | Earnings, spending, recovery, and time-to-aspiration by play pattern. | Whether rewards preserve meaningful choices. |
| Reliability | Time to playable input, failed actions, lost progress, reconnect recovery, mobile performance. | Whether technical friction is defeating the design. |
| AI usefulness and cost | Human outcomes aided, model failures, cost per meaningful returning human. | Whether agents justify their operational cost. |
| Player experience | Voluntary “wanted to return” versus “felt obliged” feedback and observed choices. | Whether a metric gain represents a better game. |

Do not choose universal retention targets from unrelated games. Establish Allworld's current cohort sizes, consent coverage, guest/account linking, device mix, and baseline first. Analytics here are partly client-reported and consent-dependent; document those limits. Known agent provenance helps, but does not prove every remaining browser event is human.

Test one material variable at a time. First compare clearer progress presentation using the same rewards. Then compare meaningful mission variety. Then test a small streak benefit against the weekly card. Randomise at the persistent player level; use group-level assignment where shared projects would contaminate individual groups. Predefine the outcome, minimum useful effect, observation window, and stopping rule using the real baseline. Small interview cohorts explain problems but do not establish a precise lift.

Watch what happens after the initial novelty and after a lapse. More claims on day one with poorer week-two return is not success. Higher session length caused by chores is not success either. Retain the mechanisms that produce enjoyable outcomes, visible history, and voluntary return.

**Decisions this research supports**

Allworld should give players a durable identity through accomplishments, a clear next action, and a small number of ongoing relationships and ambitions. Keep the existing forgiving weekly rhythm. Improve mission integrity and event measurement before increasing rewards. Build one memorable episode and one visible community outcome. Use agents to make those experiences responsive and available, while retaining honest attribution and fair human recognition.

The unresolved product choice is the first audience and ambition to emphasise: campus life, neighbourhood hosting, or local exploration. The architecture and reward principles above support all three, but the initial story, city, and acquisition promise should choose one. Current evidence does not justify expanding all paths at once.

Research scope completed here: comparative evidence, screenshot analysis, source audit, progression design, integration boundaries, prioritisation, and measurement plan. Runtime testing, player interviews, live analytics analysis, balance simulation, implementation, deployment, and the other computer's unmerged agent work remain separate work.
