# How players could earn real money on Allworld: research brief, 18 October 2026

Desk research by a helper agent; most figures come from secondary sources and are marked [unverified] where so. Not legal advice:
the licensing and tax points need a Nigerian lawyer before anything is built that moves money. The levels L0–L4 below are the ones
in [docs/REAL-VALUE.md](../../REAL-VALUE.md).

## What the evidence says

- **Live-stream gifts** (viewers buy coins, creators get "diamonds"): creators keep roughly half of what fans pay [unverified third-party
  guides]. Earnings are very top-heavy and funded by a few heavy spenders. In Nigeria the main platform banned 49,512 live sessions in one
  quarter of 2025 over sexual streams and later disrupted 86,000+ more (Guardian NG, BusinessDay). Gift economies come with gifting
  pressure, fraud with stolen cards and sexualised content. The service the owner named, use.live, could not be identified from its
  site (it shows a page titled "use.live houses" and nothing about streaming); a link or screenshot is needed.
- **Virtual-world creator pay** always comes out of real purchases, behind an age and ID gate, thresholds and delays: a large virtual
  world pays US$0.0038 per earned coin (13+, verified, thresholds); a battle game puts 40% of net shop revenue in a monthly pool for 18+
  island makers, paid 30 days after month end; a long-running virtual world has used an identity-checked subsidiary for cash-out since
  2019. One social world paid creators and still shut down on 1 June 2026: creator pay does not save a business that does not work.
- **Selling and buying**: marketplaces take 5–20% (a crafts site about 6.5% plus processing; a freelance site a flat 20%; another 20/10/5%
  tiers) [secondary]. The safe pattern: the buyer pays through a licensed processor, the seller is paid after delivery.
- **Nigerian rails**: a large processor charges ₦10 / ₦25 / ₦50 per transfer by size, plus ₦50 stamp duty from ₦10,000 (since 18 Feb
  2026); its split-payment product routes a share to a seller's subaccount at payment time, so the platform never holds a balance.
  Holding customers' money needs a licence (a mobile money operator: about ₦2B capital, per 2021 reporting); KYC tiers need BVN and NIN.
  Anti-money-laundering registration (SCUML) may apply; tax: the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (in force January 2026) taxes creator income.
- **Collapses**: a "100% in 30 days" scheme collapsed in April 2025 (the SEC called it unregistered; the ISA 2025 allows up to 10 years for
  Ponzi promoters); tap-to-earn games left most Nigerian players with under US$10. Sustainable earning: an outside buyer pays for real
  value and the earner gets a share. Ponzi-like: payouts come from new users' money, returns are promised, or people pay in to unlock.

## Earning models for Allworld

| Model | Who pays | Allworld's share | Rail | Licence / KYC | Fraud risk | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business ads and sponsored places | Businesses | 100% (it is Allworld's sale) | Allworld's own processor account | CAC, VAT, ARCON vetting | Low | High: starts revenue without paying users |
| Gigs for real money | Client | 5–15% | Split payment to the worker's subaccount | Worker BVN; lawyer on escrow | Medium | High |
| Shop and service sales (Showcase, Stores) | Buyer | 0% in the pilot, then 5–10% | Seller's own provider (already built) | Seller BVN | Medium | High, already live as link-out |
| Event tickets | Guests | 10–15% | Split payment | Host BVN | Low | High |
| Tips | Fans | 10–30% | Split payment | Cash-out KYC | High (stolen cards) | Medium, small amounts first |
| Sponsor-funded bounties and prizes | Brands | Negotiated | Bank transfer from the sponsor budget | Winner BVN or NIN | Low | High |
| Creator items (furniture, clothes) for real money | Buyers | 30% | Transfer | KYC | Medium | Later, needs volume |
| Live-stream gift economy | A few heavy spenders | ~50% | Transfer | KYC | High, and the harms above | Low |
| Game naira to cash | Nobody | none | none | Probably a licence | Very high | Never |

## How creator payouts could work (from safest to most ambitious)

1. **Sponsor-paid bounties and prizes, no balances.** A brand or Allworld funds a fixed prize first (best outfit, best event, a design
   contest); winners are paid by bank transfer 7 days after judging, BVN or NIN checked, ₦2,000 minimum. Nothing is promised that has not
   been paid in. Can start as soon as there is a sponsor.
2. **The buyer pays, the seller is paid after delivery.** Gigs, tickets, bookings and shop sales through split payments to the earner's
   own subaccount, so Allworld never holds money: 10% fee (less for tickets), paid 3 days after delivery is confirmed (7 for new sellers),
   ₦200,000 a month cap for new sellers, refunds taken from the next payout. Needs the processor's compliance team to accept the model.
3. **A creator pool from real revenue.** 30–40% of Allworld's real revenue (ads, cosmetics, tickets) shared monthly by measured
   engagement, ₦10,000 threshold, 30-day hold, 18+, full KYC, caps per creator. Only after a licensed partner holds the funds and a lawyer
   has cleared it.

## Red lines

Never promise a return or "double your money"; never ask people to pay in to unlock earning; never pay out of new users' money; never
let game naira become cash or hold users' balances without a licensed holder; no referral pyramids (referral cash only for a real sale,
from an ad budget); no payouts to under-18s; no sexualised streaming or gifting pressure; never skip KYC on a payout; never market
anything as an investment, a token or an airdrop.
