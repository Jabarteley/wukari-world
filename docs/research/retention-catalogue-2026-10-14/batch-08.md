# Batch 08 — award-winning narrative and identity games

Verified award wins were checked against organizer pages or the developer/publisher's own award record. The transfer notes are design inferences for Allworld's social city, resident, home, career and mission systems; they are not evidence that these games achieved daily live retention. Most examples are finite campaigns that earn return through attachment, memory, interpretation, mastery or replayed choices.

## Evidence and mechanics notes

Each game's JSON entry contains the exact award URL plus two concrete mechanic sources. Official award sources include AIAS/D.I.C.E., BAFTA, The Game Awards, the Canadian Game Awards, Peabody and the Independent Games Festival/GDC; mechanic sources are official developer, publisher and platform pages.

The strongest transferable patterns are: a resident's history expressed through objects and spaces; choices that remain legible after a delay; small repeatable care or mastery rituals; and social encounters that become personal memories. These patterns suggest meaningful return, but they do not prove DAU, live-service retention, or sustainable content economics for Allworld. The main risks are authored-content cost, emotional-theme sensitivity, and converting reflective mechanics into chores or collectibles.

## Verification result

All 16 entries now have an explicit organizer or official developer/publisher winner record. Chicory: A Colorful Tale and TOEM were restored from BAFTA’s 2022 winner archive; A Short Hike was restored from GDC’s official 2020 IGF winner announcement. No unresolved award claim remains in this batch.
