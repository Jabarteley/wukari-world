The transfer boundary matters. Pong, Space Invaders, Pac-Man, Tetris, and Street Fighter II support compact repeatable activities. Mario and Zelda show how a sequence of learnable spaces can create continuation without a daily timer. SimCity and Civilization show persistent projects and consequences, but their abstractions need a human layer in Allworld so residents do not become numbers. Ultima Online is useful for persistent identity and social belonging, while also carrying the strongest risks around time pressure, exclusion, and incumbent advantage.

The evidence gap is recognition, not the historical release context, for Super Mario 64 and Ultima Online. No exact-game award or induction was asserted without a primary source. Ultima Online’s mechanics are explicitly sourced to current official New Legacy documentation as legacy-context evidence, rather than presented as a verified reconstruction of every 1997 rule. **Historical game cards**

**H01. Pong — 1972**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pong/). Return pattern: replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2015; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pong/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Two-paddle ball game:** The Strong describes Pong as a simple game involving two paddles and a ball. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pong/).
- **Intuitive controls:** The museum specifically identifies Pong’s intuitive controls and satisfying gameplay as part of its lasting appeal. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pong/).
- **Repeated challenge:** The museum describes the game as inviting people to try their hand at keeping the ball bouncing as long as possible. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pong/).

**Human desires, inferred:** competition, mastery, shared play.

**Reason to return, inferred:** A clear rematch offers immediate feedback, social comparison, and a chance to improve a small skill.

**Allworld transfer:** Short two-person street or venue challenges with transparent rules, instant replay, and cosmetic recognition for mastery.

**Transfer risk:** A zero-sum score loop can exclude less-skilled or solo residents; offer cooperative and practice modes.

**H02. Space Invaders — 1978**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/space-invaders/). Return pattern: replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2016; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/space-invaders/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Horizontal laser cannon:** The player moves a cannon along the bottom of the screen and fires upward. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/space-invaders/).
- **Descending formation:** Five rows of aliens advance toward the player, making the available safe space shrink. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/space-invaders/).
- **High score and saucers:** A high-score display and occasional flying saucers add comparison and bonus targets. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/space-invaders/).

**Human desires, inferred:** mastery, tension, status.

**Reason to return, inferred:** Each attempt is short and legible: survive longer, clear more rows, and beat a visible score.

**Allworld transfer:** Compact escalating civic or work challenges with readable threat, score history, and optional personal bests.

**Transfer risk:** Escalation can become stressful or repetitive; make difficulty adjustable and avoid punitive streaks.

**H03. Pac-Man — 1980**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pac-man/). Return pattern: replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2015; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pac-man/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Maze collection:** Pac-Man navigates a maze eating pellets while routing around four ghosts. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pac-man/).
- **Power pellets:** Power pellets temporarily reverse the threat relationship and let Pac-Man chase ghosts. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pac-man/).
- **Lives and score:** The player manages limited lives while building a score through pellets, fruit, and ghosts. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/pac-man/).

**Human desires, inferred:** control, mastery, playful tension.

**Reason to return, inferred:** The same maze supports route learning, risk choices, and a quick attempt to improve the last result.

**Allworld transfer:** Map-based collection routes where residents choose safe or risky errands and learn neighbourhood patterns.

**Transfer risk:** A fixed optimal route can reduce exploration and make the world feel like a chore list.

**H04. Tetris — 1984**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/tetris/). Return pattern: replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2015; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/tetris/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Rotate and place tetrominoes:** Seven four-square shapes fall into a well and can be moved and rotated before landing. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/tetris/).
- **Line clearing:** Complete horizontal rows disappear, creating space and scoring progress. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/tetris/).
- **Speed escalation:** Pieces fall faster as play continues, tightening the decision window. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/tetris/).

**Human desires, inferred:** order, flow, mastery.

**Reason to return, inferred:** Simple rules generate endless arrangement decisions, with each run exposing a new way to improve board management.

**Allworld transfer:** Use small, combinatorial planning tasks that reward arranging limited resources without requiring a daily schedule.

**Transfer risk:** Abstract optimization may feel detached from people and place; connect tasks to meaningful local outcomes.

**H05. Super Mario Bros. — 1985**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/super-mario-bros/). Return pattern: continuation plus replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2015; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/super-mario-bros/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Side-scrolling movement and jumping:** Players guide Mario through scrolling levels using running, jumping, and momentum. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/super-mario-bros/).
- **Power-ups:** Mushrooms and other items change survivability or abilities, giving exploration and risk a visible payoff. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/super-mario-bros/).
- **World and level progression:** A sequence of Mushroom Kingdom stages builds a longer rescue journey from short attempts. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/super-mario-bros/).

**Human desires, inferred:** progress, adventure, mastery.

**Reason to return, inferred:** A failed jump teaches a concrete correction, while the next level promises new hazards and discoveries.

**Allworld transfer:** Stage neighbourhood missions as short, learnable episodes with optional discoveries and a longer resident arc.

**Transfer risk:** Linear progression can punish missed sessions and overvalue reflex skill; provide checkpoints and varied abilities.

**H06. The Legend of Zelda — 1986**

[Release/history source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/the-legend-of-zelda/). Return pattern: continuation.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2016; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/the-legend-of-zelda/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Non-linear overworld exploration:** Players are free to explore Hyrule without a fixed order while gathering eight Triforce fragments. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/the-legend-of-zelda/).
- **Item-gated discovery:** Dungeons and tools reveal new routes, puzzles, and combat options across the world. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/the-legend-of-zelda/).
- **Battery-backed saving:** The original console release used internal battery backup so a longer adventure could continue across sessions. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/the-legend-of-zelda/).

**Human desires, inferred:** curiosity, agency, mastery.

**Reason to return, inferred:** Unresolved places, clues, and newly acquired tools make the next session a question the player has chosen.

**Allworld transfer:** Let residents discover city opportunities in multiple orders, with persistent clues, relationships, and tool-gated access.

**Transfer risk:** Opaque objectives can produce confusion or reliance on spoilers; provide optional guidance without removing agency.

**H07. SimCity — 1989**

[Release/history source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/simcity/). Return pattern: continuation.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2024; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/simcity/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Open-ended city building:** Players lay roads and construct buildings while choosing their own desirable outcome rather than pursuing one required win state. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/simcity/).
- **Systemic consequences:** Population, transport, and other city conditions respond to planning decisions through an underlying simulation. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/simcity/).
- **Crisis response:** Changing problems and needs, including disasters, ask the player to diagnose and respond to a living system. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/simcity/).

**Human desires, inferred:** creation, stewardship, experimentation.

**Reason to return, inferred:** A city remembers prior choices and produces new problems, giving players a continuing project without a single authored endpoint.

**Allworld transfer:** Make neighbourhoods persistent systems where residents can build, observe consequences, and repair shared problems.

**Transfer risk:** Abstract numbers can hide who bears costs; Allworld must represent residents and affected communities, not only efficiency.

**H08. Street Fighter II — 1991**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/street-fighter-ii/). Return pattern: replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2017; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/street-fighter-ii/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Head-to-head rounds:** Two human players compete directly in short one-on-one rounds with a clear winner. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/street-fighter-ii/).
- **Distinct character styles:** Eight characters have different fighting styles and special moves, inviting comparative mastery. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/street-fighter-ii/).
- **Combos and timing:** Players learn complex signature moves and multi-hit combos that reward speed, timing, and practice. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/street-fighter-ii/).

**Human desires, inferred:** competition, identity, mastery.

**Reason to return, inferred:** A rematch can test a different character, counter a known opponent, or turn practice into visible skill.

**Allworld transfer:** Offer fair, short resident or venue competitions with role identity and practice queues before public ranking.

**Transfer risk:** Public ranking and adversarial play can produce humiliation or toxicity; preserve private, cooperative, and unranked routes.

**H09. Civilization — 1991**

[Release/history source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/sid-meiers-civilization/). Return pattern: continuation plus replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2022; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/sid-meiers-civilization/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **Turn-based empire building:** A player grows from one settler across centuries by founding cities, roads, mines, and farms. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/sid-meiers-civilization/).
- **Technology tree tradeoffs:** Only one improvement can be researched at a time, forcing choices among knowledge, units, and buildings. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/sid-meiers-civilization/).
- **Multiple victory paths:** A game can be won through conquest or by developing space travel, supporting different strategic identities. [Source](https://www.museumofplay.org/games/sid-meiers-civilization/).

**Human desires, inferred:** agency, long-term planning, meaningful achievement.

**Reason to return, inferred:** The player returns to continue a persistent project, test another civilization plan, or pursue a different victory condition.

**Allworld transfer:** Give residents long-lived city projects, transparent tradeoffs, and several legitimate ways to contribute or succeed.

**Transfer risk:** Long horizons and optimization can dominate human stories; keep residents, care, and recovery visible alongside metrics.

**H10. DOOM — 1993**

[Release/history source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/doom/). Return pattern: continuation plus replay.

Recognition: World Video Game Hall of Fame induction, 2015; retrospective museum induction. [Recognition source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/doom/).

**Documented mechanics:**

- **First-person combat:** The player navigates maze-like levels and fights demonic monsters from an embodied first-person view. [Source](https://www.worldvideogamehalloffame.org/games/doom/).

**Human desires, inferred:** adrenaline, mastery, shared competition.

**Reason to return, inferred:** Short combat encounters, campaign progress, and the possibility of a new challenge or player-made variation invite another session.

**Allworld transfer:** Use modular missions and optional cooperative scenarios, with a safe authoring layer for community-created city challenges.

**Transfer risk:** Violence and competitive intensity do not transfer directly to a social-life world; preserve agency and avoid making harm the default reward.

**Transfer risk:** Completion lists can turn exploration into checklist pressure; allow residents to stop at meaningful partial completion.
