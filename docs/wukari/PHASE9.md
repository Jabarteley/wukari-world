## 9B: simulated traffic

Original low-poly motorcycles with riders, cars, SUVs, delivery trucks and truck/trailers use real original-node adjacency. Motorcycles dominate normal density. Traffic is single-file on source centerlines, not a reconstruction of lane widths, actual flow or live traffic. Settings controls Off/Light/Normal/Busy density. All wheels rotate from traveled distance. Route choice respects mapped one-way/roundabout direction and restrictive motor-access tags; incomplete, conflicting or too-narrow source intervals are excluded. Actual access remains unknown.

Vehicles accelerate, slow at junctions, turn smoothly, stop for the player/vehicles, and test every small translation against rotated vehicle/building geometry. Player movement also rejects vehicle footprints. Long trucks accept only gentler turns; congested traffic may queue rather than invent passing lanes. Hidden-distance spawning is at least35m away, with scale fades. Stuck vehicles retire away from the player. No driving, passengers or multiplayer synchronization.

Deterministic three-minute simulated-time tests cover all five types, original-edge positions, turns, no building penetration, vehicle separation and density-off removal. Both retained rigged presets now support T-shirts, polos, lowered-hood hoodies, long jackets and casual shirts; jeans, cargo trousers, joggers, casual trousers and shorts; sneakers, boots, casual shoes and sandals. Sleeves/collars, hood, pockets, cuffs and garment lengths change geometry. Pants and top share one skinned draw, while fitted shoe shells/soles/trim are merged per foot. Native face topology, all eight hair styles, saved colors and presentation presets remain. Legacy records migrate to jeans without losing other choices.

The shoe close-up button in customization exposes every style. Visual QA rejected pointed tube-style toe caps and replaced them with rounded foot-outline shells and solid soles. Sandal feet rest on their soles. Ankle counter-rotation improves alignment during the existing gait. A new test measures actual animated shoe vertices against the ground; an initial transformed-AABB check overstated penetration, so it was corrected to test real geometry rather than relaxed. Rig weights, distinct wardrobe geometry, finite animation, persistence and vertex ground clearance are tested on both presets. Small cloth/trim seams and stylized silhouettes remain owner visual review items; no cloth simulation is claimed.
